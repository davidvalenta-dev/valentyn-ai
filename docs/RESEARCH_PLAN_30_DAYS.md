# Thirty-day research plan

Starting November 10, 2026, in America/New_York. These are dated research work packages, not scheduled automations or promised outcomes. The long-term objective remains scientific superintelligence; milestones are awarded only for evidence.

## November 10–16: establish the learned-proof baseline

Finish independent audits of the new neural artifacts. Reproduce CPU and Metal inference, checkpoint loading, wrong-proof rejection, and zero-symbolic-fallback tests. Freeze a larger generator with canonical/family/size holdouts. Run five independent model-training seeds, not just random-decoding seeds. Log total training/inference/Lean time and peak process/allocator memory. Confirm the actual configuration on an 8 GB machine before claiming 8 GB hardware validation.

Read NanoProof's policy/value objective, coupled-goal handling, and verification caveats in full; inspect LeanTree's extraction interface on a tiny pinned library subset. Study natural deduction, sequent calculus, bidirectional typing, cross-entropy, and paired experimental design. Deliver a reproducibility package and a decision about whether factorized learning has enough headroom beyond symbolic and random controls.

## November 17–23: test cumulative knowledge

Implement typed structural retrieval and exact verified theorem reuse, with a no-memory control and explicit construction/retrieval cost. Extract proof-state failure categories and small recurring subproofs. Verify generalized macro candidates before reuse. Compare exact-match caching, randomly selected macros, and cost-selected abstractions on held-out composition depth. Reject the abstraction hypothesis if gains are only renamed lookup or disappear after charging library costs.

Study unification, term substitution, proof normalization, theorem dependency graphs, and minimum-description-length objectives. Deliver reusable certified artifacts plus a controlled transfer experiment; do not expand the library simply because storage is available.

## November 24–30: add a second mathematical domain

Introduce a typed natural-number or finite-combinatorial construction language with an independently reviewed formal semantics. Use exact witnesses and Lean proofs; numerical fitting alone must not be counted as proof. Keep the same learned-action/evaluation interfaces. Compare reuse across domains against training each domain separately. Test a policy-only model before adding a value head; then ablate the value head under matched total training/search cost.

Study induction, algebraic normalization, constructive witnesses, AND/OR search, and value calibration. Deliver a frozen two-domain benchmark and an informative positive or negative transfer result.

## December 1–7: evaluate controlled algorithmic improvement

Extend the mutation representation only where profiling and oracle baselines reveal headroom: action ordering, lemma retrieval thresholds, and budget allocation. Keep executable evaluator and corpus immutable. Include random-search and hand-tuned controls, account for proposal/selection cost, preserve diverse candidate configurations, and run independent seeds. Generated source code remains disabled until an actual OS-level sandbox is implemented and tested.

Study evolutionary search, sequential testing, selection bias, distribution shift, and capability regressions. Deliver full lineage and held-out results. Zero promotions or a dominated compute frontier is a result to preserve, not a reason to weaken the gate.

## December 8–9: scientific decision and next resource request

Freeze the pilot artifacts, write a concise technical report, document statement fidelity and novelty status, and decide which hypotheses survive. Release failed controls alongside successes. Identify whether additional compute would test an evidenced bottleneck or merely scale an unproven idea. Do not claim an open mathematical contribution without literature review and independent qualified review.

## First potential publication

**Question:** Can certified local-state learning and cost-selected abstraction reuse improve out-of-family proof construction on small hardware beyond legality-matched controls?

**Dataset:** newly frozen propositional generators with family/size holdouts, followed by an independently designed arithmetic/construction domain. Historical development sets are excluded from confirmatory results.

**Method:** five independent training seeds; frozen checkpoints; complete budget/latency/memory records; independent Lean rechecks. Compare whole-term models, state policies, retrieval, symbolic search, untrained/random policies, and abstraction/no-abstraction variants. Use paired confidence intervals and family-level resampling, not just theorem-level significance on correlated templates.

**Ablations:** legality information, conjunction projection, decomposition supervision, parameter count, training tokens, search width, value prediction, and library lookup/creation cost. A new method must improve the Pareto frontier, not only success at a higher budget.

**Contribution threshold:** a substantively new abstraction or credit-assignment mechanism with replicated transfer and cost gains, plus reproducible artifacts. A small known-method reproduction or one synthetic score increase is insufficient by itself.

## Beyond the Mac

Local work covers corpus/proof auditing, small policies, typed environments, modest benchmark generation, retrieval, and controlled configuration experiments. If CPU elaboration dominates a successful setup, a university CPU allocation can increase independent Lean throughput without changing the model. If a learned policy/value hypothesis succeeds but data/parameter scaling is the bottleneck, profile a 24–48 GB accelerator experiment with a fixed token budget before requesting a longer allocation. Full NanoProof-scale pretraining/RL needs a separately costed multi-accelerator plan; do not infer its cost from tiny-model timings. A larger pretrained model may be an optional proposal instrument without replacing the independent from-scratch track.

The supplied brief's later stages remain contingent: learned reasoning → algorithmic improvement → cumulative discovery → independently reviewed novel mathematics → cross-domain research → stronger general scientific reasoning → major open-problem programs. None follows automatically from the previous stage's software existing.

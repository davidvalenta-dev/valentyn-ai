# Research landscape

Reviewed November 10, 2026. This is a targeted primary-source review, not an exhaustive survey. Results below are authors' reported findings, not reproduced by Valentyn. Bottlenecks and proposed responses are our analysis. Public code availability does not establish full reproducibility: weights, datasets, services, compute, and toolchain versions also matter.

## Formal mathematical reasoning

| Approach / primary sources | What works | Bottleneck and reproduction opportunity | Falsifiable next question |
|---|---|---|---|
| [AlphaProof](https://research.google/pubs/olympiad-level-formal-mathematical-reasoning-with-reinforcement-learning/) | Reinforcement learning and formal verification turn proof search into a task with trustworthy success feedback; problem-specific test-time adaptation expands search. | Sparse rewards, formalization, and adaptation cost remain. Reproduce small search/RL loops; the reviewed publication does not supply a ready local reproduction of the complete training system. | Does learning a search policy beat a strong fixed symbolic strategy at equal total compute on new theorem families? |
| [DeepSeek-Prover-V2 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21801), [code and models](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-Prover-V2) | Recursive decomposition supplies intermediate proof goals, combined with reinforcement learning. | Bad decompositions can multiply work; sampled proof counts and model size complicate comparisons. Public releases make proof generation experiments possible, while large-scale training is a different experiment. | Does a predicted decomposition-value model reduce verified proof cost beyond simply spending the same budget on independent attempts? |
| [Goedel-Prover-V2 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03613), [repository](https://github.com/Goedel-LM/Goedel-Prover-V2) | Scaffolded data synthesis, Lean-guided corrections, and model averaging address difficulty and proof diversity. | Self-generated curricula may cover only familiar structures; specialization can hide transfer failures. Public models, data, and code support controlled correction and curriculum ablations. | Does a structural holdout preserve correction gains after excluding renamed and near-duplicate tasks? |
| [Advancing Mathematics Research with AI-Driven Formal Proof Search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22763), [released proof results](https://github.com/google-deepmind/alphaproof-nexus-results) | Authors report resolving 9/353 open Erdős problems and proving 44/492 OEIS conjectures. A simpler generation/verification agent reproduced the Erdős successes at higher cost on difficult cases. | Verification is distinct from faithful formalization and novelty. Released proof artifacts permit checking selected results; they do not reproduce all proprietary model inference or agent training. | On a frozen research task set, which mechanism improves verified discoveries per dollar over a simple generation/check baseline? |

Research-level formal proof search already has published evidence. It would be inaccurate to describe all AI mathematics as merely competition solving. It would also be inaccurate to infer general scientific intelligence from a small number of mathematical successes.

## Discovery, synthesis, and self-improvement

| Approach / primary sources | What works | Bottleneck and reproduction opportunity | Falsifiable next question |
|---|---|---|---|
| [AlphaEvolve](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13131), [verification examples](https://github.com/google-deepmind/alphaevolve_results) | Evolutionary program search combines model proposals with objective evaluation, producing algorithmic and mathematical constructions. | Requires a useful evaluator and a searchable representation; improving one objective can conceal broader regression. Released constructions can be checked; they are not the complete production search system. | Can proof-certified reusable constructions transfer to unseen sizes without the cost of rediscovery? |
| [Darwin Gödel Machine](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22954), [code](https://github.com/jennyzzt/dgm) | Maintains an archive of modified agents and evaluates improvements empirically. | Benchmark improvement is not a proof of indefinitely compounding improvement; selection can overfit and execution needs isolation. Public code enables small mutation/evaluation experiments. | Does immutable, family-held-out evaluation retain gains when mutation and selection costs are charged? |
| [Hyperagents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19461), revised November 1, 2026 | Extends self-modification to the mechanism that proposes improvements, reporting gains across tasks. | Editable evaluation machinery and task/meta-level credit assignment create confounding risks. Begin with a fixed verifier and mutable proposal policies, then separately test meta-level changes. | Does evolving the mutation operator help on unseen domains compared with a fixed operator under matched budgets? |
| [DreamCoder](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.08381) | Alternates program synthesis, symbolic library growth, and learned search guidance. | Useful abstractions must amortize discovery and retrieval cost; a growing library can make search worse. Small typed DSLs permit inexpensive library-learning replications. | Do proof-certified macros reduce total search cost on held-out composition depths after accounting for library construction? |
| [Absolute Zero](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03335) | Generates executable tasks and learns from verifiable self-play outcomes. | “Zero data” concerns the post-training task source, not a model created without pretraining. A self-generated curriculum can neglect useful domains. | Does external transfer improve when curricula are rewarded for learning progress on separate task families rather than immediate self-play success? |

## Search, representations, and architectures

| Approach / primary sources | What works | Bottleneck and reproduction opportunity | Falsifiable next question |
|---|---|---|---|
| [HyperTree Proof Search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11491), [Evariste](https://github.com/facebookresearch/Evariste) | AND/OR proof structure and online training allocate work across interdependent subgoals. | A proof requiring all subgoals is unlike a single-path game. Value estimates, branching, and invalid proposals can dominate. Reproduce bounded search comparisons before training a large policy. | Does a graph-aware budget controller outperform iterative deepening and restarts when all proof-generation and verification costs are included? |
| Neural-symbolic knowledge representation: [DreamCoder](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.08381) and [HTPS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11491) | Typed programs, symbolic dependencies, and learned guidance constrain the search space while retaining adaptable heuristics. | Formal representations can lose informal intent; neural rankings need reliable training targets. Valentyn initially keeps the symbolic rules and learns only strategy selection. | Does typed structural representation improve family-held-out performance beyond token-based similarity at equal parameter count? |
| [Titans](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00663) | Explores persistent neural memory with test-time updates alongside attention. | Memory retention and long-context benchmarks do not establish proof planning or stable continual learning. A small recurrent-memory policy is a testable component, not a complete general intelligence architecture. | Do verified retrieval targets improve mathematical transfer without catastrophic forgetting or an oversized memory advantage? |
| [Mamba-2 / structured state-space duality](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.21060), [implementation](https://github.com/state-spaces/mamba) | Structured state-space models offer efficient alternatives/hybrids to attention. | Sequence efficiency does not imply discovery ability; official fast kernels are a separate portability concern for an M2 prototype. Compare small portable recurrent, graph, and attention policies on identical proof traces first. | Is generalization better at matched parameters, training tokens, and end-to-end compute, rather than just longer context? |
| [Awakening the Sleeping Agent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08388) | Authors report that small amounts of Lean-specific agentic data restore tool-use ability suppressed by specialization. | A stronger theorem prover can lose general capabilities; narrow success is an insufficient continual-learning measure. | Can a frozen multi-domain regression battery detect and prevent capability loss during mathematical self-improvement? |

## Architectural conclusion

The evidence favors a small immutable correctness/evaluation core with experimentally mutable search and learning components. Eight separate engines are not initially justified. Reusable abstractions, curriculum selection, and compute allocation deserve isolated tests before integrating them. Large-scale language pretraining, bigger contexts, or an editable agent loop should not be treated as automatic routes to generality.

Current Valentyn reproduces only a small symbolic search/verified feedback/policy-selection pattern. It does not reproduce any named frontier system. The strongest immediate competing explanation for its measured node reduction is simply choosing a better constant strategy; the benchmark includes that control.
